Out of Water, an original IP

A 3D platformer collectathon built for two players on one sofa, in splitscreen, starring a turtle and a seagull. I own the character and enemy gameplay code, and I work in engine across:

Gameplay

  • Component driven state machines for both playable characters and the enemies, rebuilt from single file controllers
  • The co-op moveset that hangs off them, and the game feel around it
  • The splitscreen plumbing underneath co-op: per player device routing, cameras and character swapping

Performance & tooling

  • GPU driven rendering, quality tiers, LOD and culling passes, measured branch to branch with a profiling tool I wrote for the team
  • Blockout interactables and editor tooling the level designers build puzzles from
  • Supporting systems the rest of the team works through, including collectable saving and an in-game debug console

Presentation & audio

  • A UI Toolkit front end on a shared token system: HUD, character select, pause, save slots and a controls tutorial
  • Water, caustics and bubble shaders, in Shader Graph and in hand written URP HLSL
  • A decoupled FMOD layer the audio team plugs into

About

A bit about me

I'm Oliver, a games developer working in Unity and C#. I've been making games in Unity since 2018, and building them consistently since 2023, so when I graduated from Teesside University in July 2026 with First Class Honours in BSc (Hons) Games Development, the degree sat on top of years of self-driven projects.

The part of games I enjoy most is the systems layer: state machines driving characters and enemy AI, and the profiling and optimisation that lets a scene keep its frame budget once everything is running at once. My final-year project artefact, Cyber Station, scored 90 out of 100.

Most of what's below is playable in the browser on itch.io, and nearly all of it has source on GitHub. If you'd rather read about the engineering, the project breakdowns go deeper on eight of the nine.

Cyber Station

Apr 2026

My final year project: a neon station management game about building a transport hub that can take the pressure. Four systems pull against each other, so every layout decision changes how passengers path through the space, which moves the ratings, which gates what you can build next.

  • Placement a full build catalogue, each item costed and carrying a stat effect
  • Passenger flow crowds navigating the concourse you laid out
  • Ratings a star score read off how well the station actually copes
  • Progression tiers that unlock against that rating

Train scheduling, self dispatching staff AI, an economy and save/load sit around that core.

LinkedIn · 7 May 2026

I'm excited to share that I've finished my final year project: Cyber Station! 🚆 Cyber Station is a 3D construction and management simulation made in Unity, where the player designs, expands, and operates a growing futuristic train station. The project became a really rewarding systems-design challenge, bringing together passenger AI, facility interactions, train scheduling, a grid-based build system, station ratings, decoration coverage, progression, and save/load functionality. For the one-minute demo, I wanted to show the station as a living simulation: passengers moving through the station, trains arriving, facilities being used, and the management systems feeding into each other. This has been a huge step forward for me as a games developer. It pushed me to build more scalable Unity systems, work with finite state machine AI, use ScriptableObjects for data-driven content, and structure a larger codebase around managers, controllers, coordinators, and reusable components.
